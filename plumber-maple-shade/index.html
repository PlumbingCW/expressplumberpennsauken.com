Winter in Maple Shade: frozen lines and split pipes

Cold snaps are the classic emergency. Water expands as it freezes, and a pipe in an unheated crawl space, garage wall or outside faucet can split without warning. The leak often appears only after the thaw, when water starts to flow again.

If you suspect a frozen line, open the nearest faucet, shut the main and leave the thawing to slow, safe heat such as a hair dryer. Never use an open flame. Once a pipe has split, call for burst pipe repair before the water does more damage.

Spring: thaw, storms and sump pumps

Heavy spring rain tests every basement. A sump pump that sat idle all winter may stick, and a pump without battery backup stops when the power does. If water is entering, keep clear of outlets and the panel, and call us about a sump pump that will not start or will not shut off.

A pump that starts is not proof that the discharge line is clear. Check that water is leaving the pipe outside, away from the foundation.

Summer: heavy use and cloudy water

Summer puts more demand on the water system. In August 2016, residents reported cloudy, discolored water and the township responded by flushing hydrants across town. If the cloudiness is on cold taps only and clears after a few minutes, check with Maple Shade Utilities first.

If a low-pressure problem is only in your house, the cause may be your service line. A water line repair call is appropriate when pressure falls and neighbors are fine.

Fall: water heaters and boilers

The first cold nights reveal tired water heaters and boilers. A tank that rumbles, leaks at the base or runs out of hot water quickly is warning you. For a boiler that will not fire or a radiator that leaks, call before the coldest weeks. An emergency in January is harder on everyone than a visit in October, and same-day appointments are offered when a plumber is available.

Maple Shade Utilities supplies the township's water

Maple Shade Utilities pumps water from five wells in the Potomac-Raritan-Magothy aquifer, between 126 and 500 feet deep. The raw water is high in iron, so it is treated by aeration, filtration and settling before it reaches your tap. The utility also buys some water from New Jersey American Water. Its line, 856-488-7450, is staffed around the clock for water and sewer emergencies.

In December 2025 the township closed a New Jersey Water Bank loan for lead and galvanized service line replacement. The township counts about 176 known galvanized services and about 330 of unknown material, some of which may contain lead.
