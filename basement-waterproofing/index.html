What plumbing can and cannot do about a wet basement

Water enters a basement three ways: through the floor, through the walls, or up from a drain. Plumbing deals with the first and third by collecting water and moving it out. It does not repair cracked foundation walls, regrade a yard or fix gutters. The same scope applies when we serve Cherry Hill homes next door.

Plumbing handles

  • Interior perimeter drains that collect water at the footing
  • Sump pits and pumps that move it out
  • Backwater valves that stop sewage coming up
  • Floor drains and their traps
  • Discharge piping away from the house

Other trades handle

  • Downspouts, gutters and yard grade
  • Cracks in foundation walls
  • Exterior sealing and excavation
  • Window wells and masonry repair
  • Waterproof coatings on walls

Fixing downspouts and grade is often the cheapest step and should come first. If you have heavy runoff against the wall, no pump can fully offset it. A related situation is covered on our sump pump page.

Interior perimeter drains and sump systems

An interior drain is a perforated pipe set in gravel along the inside edge of the basement floor. Water that rises at the footing flows into it and runs to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside.

The system is only as good as its pump and discharge. A backup pump, an alarm and a clean check valve make it more dependable, and the pit should be inspected each year for silt.

Backwater valves and floor drains

A basement floor drain connects to the same sewer system as your toilets. During heavy rain, the public sewer can fill and push water back up the lowest drain it finds. A backwater valve closes when flow reverses and keeps that water out of the house.

A valve must be accessible for cleaning, and it does not help with groundwater. Floor drains also need water in their traps, or sewer gas can rise. Pour a little water into rarely used drains each month.

Discharge: where the pumped water goes

Pumped water has to leave the house and flow away from the foundation. If it empties near the wall, it returns to the pit and the pump runs all day.

Tying a sump discharge into the sanitary sewer is a decision for the Construction Code Department and the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority, not for a homeowner or plumber alone. Ask them first.

Finishing a basement or building new

If you are finishing a basement, solve water first. Drywall and flooring over a wet slab grow mold and lose their value. In new construction, plan the pit, floor drain and any backwater valve before the slab is poured, because adding them later means cutting concrete.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Ready.gov and the EPA flood guidelines both recommend check valves on drains and a sump pump with battery backup for flood-prone basements. Plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the Pennsauken Construction Code Department enforces locally, so any new drain or sump work should be permitted.
