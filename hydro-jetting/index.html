Emergency backups and repeat clogs: when jetting is the answer

A cable cutting head opens a channel the width of its blade. Whatever clings to the rest of the pipe wall stays, and it starts narrowing the line again the day after. That is why the same kitchen branch or main can clog every few months.

Jetting is the better choice when the blockage is a coating rather than a plug. It is also the first tool to consider when a line has backed up on a weekend and a previous snaking only bought a short break.

Grease and soap film

Fat from kitchens and laundry cools into a paste that a cable carves through but does not remove. Hot water at pressure strips it off the walls.

Mineral scale

Hard deposits build up inside older lines and reduce the bore. A jetter with the right nozzle can polish them back.

Tree roots

Fine root hair that enters at joints can be cut and flushed out, but the opening where they got in still needs a repair.

Repeat clogs on one line

If the same stoppage returns, the pipe wall is the problem, not the single clog.

How a jetting visit goes, step by step

Phone triage and access check

We ask which drains are affected, where the cleanout is and whether the line has been cabled before. That decides what equipment goes on the truck.

Camera look first

A push camera on a flexible rod shows the pipe condition before any water is pushed through. This is where we spot a collapse or offset that would rule jetting out.

Jetting pass

The hose is fed through the cleanout with a nozzle that throws jets backward to pull itself forward and scour the wall. We work in passes until the water runs clear.

Camera look after

A second run confirms the wall is clean and shows any remaining crack, root entry point or low spot. You see the recorded result before the visit ends.

What jetting cannot fix

Water does not repair a broken pipe. A collapsed section, a separated joint, or a belly where the pipe sags and holds water will clog again no matter how clean the walls are. In those cases the camera result points to repair, and our sewer line repair page explains the options.

Old cast iron needs extra care. Homes in Pennsauken, NJ with a median build year near 1964 often have cast iron drain lines, and heavy scale can be holding a thin wall together. We look at the pipe on camera first and may choose a lower pressure or a cable instead if the walls look fragile.

Good fit for jetting

  • Grease built up on kitchen and laundry branches
  • Slow main line with a sound pipe on camera
  • Root hair at joints, followed by a repair plan
  • Lines that were cabled and clogged again soon after

Not a fit, call for repair

  • Collapsed or crushed pipe
  • Offset or separated joints
  • Cracked cast iron with a thin wall
  • A belly that holds standing water

Grease trap cleaning for commercial kitchens

Restaurants, delis and other food businesses in Pennsauken, NJ, including those along US 130 and Route 73, depend on their grease traps and kitchen lines running clean. A full trap pushes fat into the sewer lateral, and a backup during service means closing the line. We clean kitchen branches by jetting and can check the trap and the line behind it on camera.

Rules on grease interceptors and who services them are set locally, so ask the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority and your hauler what applies to your building. Jetting is priced by the work involved, which varies a good deal between a short kitchen branch and a long main.
