When a failing sewer line is an emergency

Sewage rising in the basement, a sinkhole or soft patch along the pipe route, or toilets that fail the moment someone runs the washer all point to a line that has stopped working. Some failures are slow, but a collapse can turn a slow drain into a full backup in a day.

If you smell sewage in a crawl space or see wastewater outdoors, avoid contact and keep children and pets away. Weekend and overnight breakdowns are treated as urgent, and we offer same-day appointments when the schedule permits.

Belly or sag

A low section holds water and solids, so toilets flush slowly and clogs repeat after every cleaning.

Offset joint

Two pipe ends no longer line up, which catches paper and lets roots in.

Crack or break

Soil and water enter the pipe, and sewage leaks into the ground, sometimes producing a soft or sunken area.

Collapse

The pipe has crushed in. Nothing passes, and cleaning tools stop short at the same spot.

Repair routes compared: spot repair, lining or dig and replace

The right repair depends on how long the damaged section is, the pipe material, and whether the shape is still round enough for a liner. A camera inspection comes first because it shows all of those things. Lining in particular cannot be quoted blind, since the liner has to pass through a clean, open pipe.

Cast iron sewer pipe and the age of Pennsauken homes

Pennsauken, NJ has a median year built near 1964, with the oldest homes in the south of the township and newer ones toward the northeast. Houses of that era often have cast iron or clay in the building drain or lateral. Cast iron rusts and flakes from the inside, which narrows the pipe and can leave the wall thin at the bottom.

When we find rough, scaled cast iron on camera, the choice is between cleaning and monitoring, lining a sound section, or replacing it. A careful look at wall condition matters here, since a harsh cleaning pass can finish off a pipe that was barely holding.

Tree roots in the sewer line

Roots follow moisture and enter through any joint or crack in the line. They show up as slow drains, repeat clogs, and a backup after wet weather. Cutting roots clears the flow for a time, but the opening stays. Repair means sealing the joint, lining the section, or replacing the pipe, and our sewer line cleaning service handles the clearing that comes first.

Public sewer projects in Pennsauken, NJ and your lateral

In August 2024 the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority closed a 24 million dollar NJ Water Bank loan to separate combined sewers and separate water and sewer mains in Pennsauken. That work concerns the public mains in the street, not the private lateral from your house. We do not know how it affects any individual property, so ask the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority where public responsibility ends and yours begins before you commit to a repair.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Trenchless lining of sewer pipe is commonly specified to ASTM F1216 for inversion cure-in-place liners and ASTM F1743 for pull-in-place liners, so ask any lining contractor which standard the liner meets. Plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the Pennsauken Construction Code Department enforces locally.
