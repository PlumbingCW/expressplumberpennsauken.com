When a water heater problem is an emergency

Some faults can wait for a morning appointment. Water spreading from the tank across a finished floor cannot. Neither can steam or hot water pouring from the relief valve pipe, scorched wiring at the unit, or a rotten-egg or gas smell near the burner.

Tanks often give out overnight, when the house is quiet and nobody is watching the floor. The 24/7 line is answered at any hour, and after-hours or weekend visits are offered when the schedule allows. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.

Shut-off steps while you wait for the plumber

Cut the fuel or power

On a gas unit, turn the gas control to off, but only if there is no gas smell. On an electric unit, switch off its breaker, and only if the panel area is dry and you can reach it safely.

Close the cold supply

Find the valve on the cold pipe entering the top of the heater. A lever handle shuts when it sits across the pipe, and a round wheel handle closes clockwise.

Relieve pressure upstairs

Open one hot faucet at the highest fixture in the house. This lets air in so the tank stops pushing water out of the leak.

Contain the water

Move belongings off the floor and use towels or a bucket. Do not step into standing water that reaches outlets, cords or the electrical panel.

Water heater leak: where the water is really coming from

A puddle under the heater does not always mean a dead tank. Fittings, valves and condensation can all put water on the floor, and each one has a different fix. Wipe the unit dry, then press a dry paper towel to each spot and see which one gets wet first.

The difference matters because a leaking connection is a short repair, while a leaking tank body cannot be patched. The wrong guess costs you either a needless new heater or another flood.

Tank body or base

Water seeping from the bottom, seams or the jacket, often with rust streaks. This points to a failed inner tank.

Supply connections

Drips at the pipes and flex connectors on top. These are usually tightened, re-sealed or replaced.

Relief valve and discharge pipe

Water or steam from the pipe beside the unit. The valve may be worn, or the tank may be running too hot or too high in pressure. Turn off the fuel or power and shut the cold supply, then call.

Drain valve at the bottom

A slow drip from the spigot near the floor. Sediment can hold it open, and plastic valves wear out.

No hot water: working through gas and electric faults in order

When the taps run cold, start with the energy source, then the heating parts, then the safety trips. Most no-hot-water calls come down to a tripped breaker, a failed element, a dead thermostat, or a gas burner that will not stay lit.

The table follows the order a plumber would test. It saves you from guessing, and it shows what is safe to look at before you call.

Repair by heater type: gas, electric and heat pump hybrid

Gas units

A pilot that keeps dropping out usually means a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot assembly, or a draft pulling the flame off. Poor venting is the one to take seriously, because combustion gases then spill into the room. Never relight a unit that has already failed twice without a technician looking at the flue.

Electric units

Two heating elements and two thermostats do the work. A failed lower element gives you short hot showers, while a failed upper element often means no hot water at all. The tank must be drained below the element before it comes out, so this is a plumber's job rather than a quick swap.

Heat pump hybrids

Hybrid units add a compressor and fan on top of the tank, with electric elements as backup. Early checks are the air filter, the condensate drain and clearance around the intake. Error codes differ by maker, so have the model name ready when you call.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Gas-fired storage heaters are listed to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard, which is why a plumber replaces safety parts such as the relief valve with listed ones rather than improvising. According to the manufacturer's manual for your model, any burner or venting work needs the exact approved parts.
