Emergency water heater replacement: when a leaking tank cannot wait

A tank that leaks from its body or base has corroded through on the inside. No sealant or new part fixes that, and the leak only grows. Water that appears at night or over a weekend still needs a response, because a full tank can empty onto a floor in a surprisingly short time.

Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see. If the unit can be isolated, the plumber can plan a same-day or next-available replacement. If it is flooding fast, shut the main water valve too and treat it like a burst pipe until help arrives.

Hot water gone with no puddle is a different story. That is often a failed element, thermostat or burner part, and the unit may be worth saving. Our water heater repair page covers that diagnosis, and the table below shows when the balance tips.

To learn how old the heater is, look for the serial number on the data plate. Many makers encode the manufacture date in it. Check the date code on the data plate and ask your plumber to read it with you, since each brand writes it differently.

Sizing a replacement for your household

Swapping like for like is common, but it is not always right. A household that has grown, or added a soaking tub, may have outgrown the old tank. One that has shrunk may be paying to heat water it never uses.

People and bathrooms

More residents and more bathrooms raise the demand on a single tank.

Peak use

Two showers plus a dishwasher at once is the real test, not the daily average.

Space and clearances

The new unit must fit the closet or alcove, with room for venting, a pan and a discharge line.

Existing connections

Gas line size, flue condition and the electrical circuit all limit what can go in.

Fuel options: gas, electric or heat pump hybrid

  • Gas usually recovers quickly and uses the existing flue and gas line, if both pass inspection.
  • Electric needs a suitable circuit and no venting, which suits closets and finished spaces.
  • Heat pump hybrid pulls heat from surrounding air, so it needs open air space, a condensate drain and a cooler, damp spot to be comfortable with.
  • Fuel changes such as gas to hybrid mean new circuits, drains or vent work. That is a planned job, covered on our water heater installation page.

If on-demand heat appeals to you, our tankless service page explains the gas line, venting and flow-rate questions that come with it.

Permits for a water heater swap in Pennsauken, NJ

Plumbing permits in the township are handled by the Construction Code Department at 2400 Merchantville Ave. Township code requires permits for construction work except ordinary repairs, so ask the department whether your particular swap needs one before the day is booked.

The licensed plumber doing the job applies for it, and the department may inspect afterward. Fees change, so see the township fee schedule rather than relying on any number you read elsewhere.

What replacement day looks like

Phone triage and price

The dispatcher asks about the unit, its location and the symptoms. A clear price is given before work starts.

Shut down and drain

The plumber turns off the fuel or power and the cold supply, then drains the old tank.

Remove and haul away

The old unit is disconnected and taken out, so you are not left with a rusted tank in the garage.

Set and connect

The new heater goes in with fresh connectors, a proper relief valve and discharge line, and venting or wiring as required.

Test and walk through

The plumber fires up the unit, checks for leaks and shows you the shutoffs and the temperature setting.

If a gas heater is the problem and you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Only call us once you are outside and the area is safe.

The old tank is scrap metal, and the plumber can haul it off for you. Ask that it be written into the price so removal is settled before work starts.

Clear the way before the plumber arrives

A little prep keeps the swap short.

  • Clear a path and about three feet around the heater
  • Locate the cold shutoff and the breaker or gas control
  • Photograph the data plate and any labels
  • Move stored items out of the closet or corner
  • Tell the dispatcher about stairs, low ceilings or tight turns

Standards and rules behind this advice

Gas storage water heaters are built to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard, and federal minimum efficiency standards from the US Department of Energy apply to new units. Plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the Pennsauken Construction Code Department enforces locally, so ask your plumber how the permit and inspection will be handled.
