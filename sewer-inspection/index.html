What a sewer camera inspection shows you

The inspection runs a small waterproof camera through the pipe from a cleanout to the street connection. The picture goes to a monitor and is recorded, with a distance count so each defect has a position. What you get is evidence of the pipe condition, not an opinion from a cleaning visit.

Pipe material and size

Cast iron, clay, plastic or older fiber pipe each wear in a different way and have different repair options.

Roots and buildup

Root hair at joints, grease coats and scale are visible and measurable.

Cracks and breaks

Hairline cracks, missing pieces and open joints show as dark gaps or soil in the frame.

Sags and offsets

A belly appears as standing water, and an offset as a visible step between pipe ends.

How the inspection works

Find the access point

We use the main cleanout. If there is none, a toilet pull or roof vent can serve as an entry, though it takes longer.

Push the camera

A push camera on a flexible rod moves down the line. Smaller lines take a smaller head so the view stays clear.

Locate with a transmitter

The camera carries a sonde, a small transmitter in the head. A hand locator above ground picks up its signal and marks the pipe route and depth on the surface.

Review and report

We stop at each defect, note the distance and depth, and save the video. You get the recording and a written summary of what we saw.

Buying a home in Pennsauken, NJ: why to check the lateral first

The median year built in Pennsauken is around 1964, with the oldest housing in the south of the township. Homes of that era often have clay or cast iron laterals, and some may have older fiber pipe. A lateral can fail long before the house shows signs, and a replacement is one of the larger repairs a new owner can face.

Ask your agent to include a sewer camera inspection in the due diligence period. The recording gives you a basis for negotiating repairs, a credit, or walking away. The township handles plumbing permits, so check with the Construction Code Department at 2400 Merchantville Ave if a seller says work was done.

When to schedule an inspection

  • After two or more backups or slow-drain episodes in a year.
  • Before you buy or sell a home with an older sewer line.
  • Before lining a pipe, since the liner needs an open, mapped route.
  • Before a driveway, patio or large tree work over the lateral.
  • When a damp spot or sinking area appears over the line route.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Camera inspections record the pipe condition so a repair choice rests on evidence. Lining work that follows an inspection is commonly specified to ASTM F1216, and plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the Pennsauken Construction Code Department enforces locally.
