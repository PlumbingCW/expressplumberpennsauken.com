When a leaking or dead disposal is an emergency

A slow drip at a hose clamp can wait for a morning appointment. Water running across the cabinet floor cannot, because it soaks the particleboard, reaches the dishwasher connection and can drip into the room below.

Treat it as an emergency when water keeps coming, when the sink backs up into the dishwasher, or when the unit smells burnt. Shut the cold supply under the sink if it is reachable, and turn off the disposal switch and breaker before anything else.

Same-day visits are possible when the schedule allows, and the 24/7 line is answered at night and on weekends too. If the problem is only a stubborn clog downstream of the unit, our drain cleaning crew handles that side of the trap.

Jam, hum or leak: three failures that look alike

The jam

Nothing happens or you hear a single click. A spoon handle, fruit pit or bone has locked the grinding plate. This is often a short fix once the power is off.

The hum

A steady drone with no spinning means the motor has power but the plate is stuck. If the hum continues after clearing the jam, the motor or its start capacitor may be finished.

The silent unit

No sound and no hum can mean a tripped reset button, a dead outlet or a failed motor. The reset button and the outlet are checked first because they cost nothing to rule out.

The leak

Water at the top flange points to the sink seal. Water from the side points to the dishwasher hose or the discharge elbow. Water from the bottom usually means a cracked housing, which ends the unit's life.

Safe reset steps before you call

Cut the power first

Flip the wall switch off and turn off the breaker for the circuit. If the disposal plugs into an outlet under the sink, unplug it. Never put a hand into the drain opening, even when it looks dead.

Look for a stuck item with a light

Shine a flashlight down the opening. Lift out anything visible using tongs or pliers, never fingers.

Turn the plate with a hex key

Most units have a hex socket in the center of the underside. Work a hex key back and forth until the plate turns freely. The owner's manual for your model shows the socket size.

Press the red reset button

The reset button sits on the bottom of the housing. Press it once, restore power, and run cold water as you test. If it trips again, stop and call.

Humming but not spinning?

Leave the unit off and talk to a licensed plumber about the next step. A short phone triage tells you whether it is a jam or a motor.

Repair or replace: how the decision usually goes

Age and location of the failure decide most of these jobs. A jam on a sound unit is a repair. A cracked housing or a rusted-through bottom is a replacement every time, since no seal will hold on a split shell.

Double-sink hookups with a dishwasher inlet and drain

A double bowl sink adds a second drain, a tailpiece and a trap arm that has to meet the disposal discharge at the right height. The disposal drains one bowl, and the other bowl connects to the same trap arm through a continuous waste.

The dishwasher drain hose attaches to a small inlet on the side of the disposal. On a new unit, that inlet is sealed by a plastic knockout plug that must be driven out before the hose goes on. A forgotten plug is one of the most common reasons a dishwasher will not empty after an install.

If you bought a unit yourself and the connections are not lining up, call for the hookup alone. We can also reinstall a unit after a sink or countertop swap, or add a first-time disposal where the kitchen never had one, which may need an outlet and a switch from an electrician.

What should never go down the disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat: they cool and coat the drain line instead of washing away.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in bulk: they pack into a paste in the trap.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels: they swell and gum up the blades and the trap arm.
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks: they jam the plate or tangle in the drain.
  • Non-food items: bottle caps, twist ties, utensils and produce stickers.

Before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of prep keeps the visit short.

  • Turn off the disposal switch and breaker.
  • Clear the cabinet under the sink so the plumber has room.
  • Note the brand and horsepower printed on the unit, if you can read it.
  • Photograph any visible leak path and the dishwasher hose.
  • Have the new unit and its box ready if you already bought one.
  • Skip the dishwasher until the drain is checked.

Worth knowing in Pennsauken

Most addresses in Pennsauken are on public sewer, so a stubborn kitchen drain rarely involves a septic tank. If a disposal clears but several fixtures drain slowly, the main line may need attention, which is a sewer line cleaning question. We also do kitchen hookups for homes in Cherry Hill.

Standards and rules behind this advice

A disposal runs on an electrical circuit, and the National Electrical Code (NEC) governs how that circuit and any hardwired connection are installed, so electrical changes belong to a licensed electrician. According to the manufacturer's installation instructions, the drain, dishwasher knockout and mounting must be done in a set order.
