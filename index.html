Start here

What counts as a plumbing emergency, and what to do first

A plumbing emergency is any problem that is damaging the house right now or making it unsafe to stay in. Water that will not stop, sewage coming up through a floor drain and a tank leaking onto the basement floor all qualify. A dripping faucet or a slow tub can usually wait for a normal appointment.

When you call our emergency plumber line, the first minute matters more than the van. The person who answers will help you find the right shut-off and decide what can safely wait.

Water that will not stop

A split pipe, a failed supply line or a gushing joint needs the main valve closed first. Our burst pipe repair crews take over once the flow is stopped.

Sewage or a full backup

If waste is rising in a tub or floor drain, stop running water and keep children and pets out of the room. A sewer line cleaning visit is usually the next step.

No hot water or a leaking tank

A tank that is weeping at the base or dumping water from the relief valve is a heater problem, not just a comfort problem. See water heater repair for what we check.

A meter that will not sit still

If every tap is off and the meter keeps turning, water is escaping somewhere. Leak detection finds the spot before walls are opened.

Do these while you wait for the plumber

These steps apply to most urgent calls and take a few minutes.

  • Find the main water shut-off and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Stop using drains if water is backing up, and keep people out of wet areas near outlets.
  • Turn off the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the path of the water.
  • Take photos of the damage for your records.
  • If you smell gas, do not touch switches. Leave and call the gas utility or 911.

Local context

Why Pennsauken homes have the plumbing problems they do

The median home in the township was built in 1964, according to American Community Survey data, and the oldest housing sits in the southern part. Homes of that era often have cast iron drains, galvanized supply pipes or early copper with soldered joints. Each material wears out in its own way, which is why a plumber asks the age of the house early in a call.

Newer construction is concentrated in the northeast, where plastic drains and newer supply lines are more likely. That does not make those homes immune.

Delair and the river side

Riverside streets near the Delaware are low and close to older mains. If water or sewage shows up in a basement here, mention it right away, since a sump pump or backwater protection may come into the discussion.

Merchantville Park and Wellwood

These older neighborhoods are where a sewer camera inspection pays off. A recorded run shows roots or a cracked clay or cast iron lateral before anyone digs.

Delaware Gardens and Jordantown

Give the dispatcher your cross streets and a nearby landmark when you call. It helps the plumber find the right address on streets that look alike.

Airport Circle and the US 130 corridor

Shops, restaurants and small warehouses along this corridor often have grease lines and floor drains. Owners should read our note on commercial plumbing before an after-hours backup.

Why a sewer backup is not always your pipe

The Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority has a loan to separate combined stormwater and sanitary sewers and water and sewer mains in Pennsauken. The goal is cleaner water and less localized flooding. We do not know when that work reaches any single street, so do not assume a backup is the street's fault or yours.

If only one drain is slow, the problem is usually in your own line. If every fixture backs up together, a sewer line repair or cleaning may be needed, and a camera run shows whether the blockage is on your side.

Most addresses get water from the Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission, which pumps from wells in the Potomac-Raritan-Magothy aquifer. A few may be on NJ American Water, so check your bill. For water hardness, ask the utility for its annual water quality report, since mineral scale on fixtures and heaters varies by address. Low pressure in a house with galvanized pipe may point to a water line repair or to repiping.
