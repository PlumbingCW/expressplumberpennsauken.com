Plumber in Delair, Pennsauken, NJ

Delair is a riverside community inside Pennsauken Township, close to the Route 90 approach to the Betsy Ross Bridge. Homes here range from single-family houses to small apartment buildings, so the first plumbing question is often who controls which shutoff.

This page explains how Delair is described by local and public sources, which utilities and permit offices apply to a Delair address, and how to describe a plumbing problem so the call goes quickly.

Where Delair sits in Pennsauken

Wikipedia lists Delair among the unincorporated communities within Pennsauken Township. It is a named community, not a separate town, so Pennsauken's permit office and township rules apply to a Delair address. Map data places the area near Route 90, the Delaware River and an active freight rail corridor.

The name also appears on the Delair Bridge, a freight rail bridge over the Delaware River that opened in 1896, and on Delair Elementary School, which serves kindergarten through grade 3 in the Pennsauken school district. Street names that appear in Delair property listings include Derousse Avenue, Sheppard Road, Tripoli Avenue, Holman Avenue and River Road.

Exact boundaries are not published in one official map, so use your street address when you call. Utilities and permits follow the address, not the neighborhood label.

Homes described in Delair

Neighborhood data profiles describe Delair as a mix of three and four bedroom single-family homes, smaller one and two bedroom homes and small apartment buildings. They say many homes were built between 1940 and 1969, some earlier and some later, and that most are owner occupied. Listing sites show homes dating from 1915 to 2008.

Water, sewer and permits for a Delair address

Most Pennsauken addresses buy water from MPWC, which pumps from wells in the Potomac-Raritan-Magothy aquifer. A few addresses may be on NJ American Water, so the bill is the quick way to confirm your provider. MPWC does not print a hardness number on the pages we reviewed, so ask the commission or test your own tap.

A township-wide sewer project to know about

In August 2024 the New Jersey Infrastructure Bank announced a 24 million dollar loan to the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority to separate a combined stormwater and sanitary sewer system in Pennsauken Township. The stated aims are better water quality and less localized flooding. The announcement does not name streets, so nothing here says Delair is or is not inside the work area.

Source: New Jersey Infrastructure Bank news release, 23 August 2024.

Plumbing services for Delair homes

Calls from Delair usually fall into a few groups. A slow or backed-up fixture calls for drain cleaning, and a repeat clog in the main line is worth a sewer camera inspection so you see the pipe before paying for a repair. A water bill that jumps or a damp wall calls for leak detection. No hot water or a tank that is dripping calls for water heater repair.

No. Wikipedia lists Delair as an unincorporated community within Pennsauken Township, so Pennsauken's township offices handle permits and local rules. Pennsauken addresses carry ZIP code 08109 or 08110.

Call your water provider. Most Pennsauken addresses are served by the Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission, which lists (856) 663-0044 for water emergencies. A few addresses are on NJ American Water, so check your bill first.

If one sink or tub is slow, the clog is almost always in that fixture's own drain. If several fixtures back up together or the floor drain gurgles, the main line is the likelier cause.

A cleanout cap on the main line is where a plumber checks first. The sewer main in the street belongs to the local sewerage authority, so ask the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority if the main itself looks blocked.

Pennsauken's township code exempts ordinary repairs from permits but requires one for most other construction work. Ask the township office at (856) 665-1000 before the work starts, because the answer depends on the job.

State rules limit plumbing work to a master plumber, an authorized contractor or their staff. Get the license number in writing, then confirm it with the state board that issues master plumber licenses.
