What this service is

We are a plumbing dispatch and repair service for Pennsauken Township and the towns around it. The office address is 7300 N Crescent Blvd, Pennsauken, NJ 08110, and the line is answered around the clock.

There is no web form and no online booking. Every request starts with a phone call, because a plumbing problem is easier to judge by talking than by typing.

The work itself is performed by licensed plumbers, and the business is insured. That is all this page claims about credentials, and the last section below shows you how to confirm it for yourself.

How a call works from start to finish

You call the 24/7 line

A person answers and asks what is happening, where the water or the backup is, and the address with ZIP code. Tell them early if you smell gas or if water is near electrical equipment.

You get safety steps first

Before anything else is scheduled, the call taker walks you through the safe next move. That might be closing the main shut-off, stopping all drain use, or leaving the house.

A plumber is arranged

We offer same-day appointments when available. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because traffic on US 130 or a job already underway can change the day.

You hear the price before work starts

The plumber looks at the problem, explains the options, and gives you a price. Work begins only after you agree to it.

How we give prices

A plumbing price depends on what is behind the wall, under the slab, or inside the tank, and nobody can see that over the phone. So the phone call is for triage and the price comes after the plumber has seen the problem.

You should hear a clear price before work starts, in plain words, with the work it covers. If the scope changes once a wall is open or a line is cleared, the plumber explains why and you decide whether to continue.

Be cautious of any company that quotes a fixed number sight unseen for a drain, a leak, or a heater. The honest answer is usually that it depends, followed by a firm price once someone has looked.

What we handle

Our work covers the problems that send people to the phone: burst pipe repair, leak detection, drain cleaning, sewer line repair, and water heater repair or replacement. We also handle toilets, faucets, disposals, sump pumps, and planned jobs such as repiping.

The full list, with a page for each service, is on the plumbing services page. If your problem is not listed, call and ask, since a plumber can usually tell you quickly whether it falls inside our work.

What we do not do

Plumbing sits next to other trades, and some jobs belong to them. We do not do structural repair, roofing, electrical work, or air conditioning and cooling systems.

If water damage has reached a ceiling joist, a roof is leaking, or a heater needs electrical work beyond the plumbing connection, we will say so and point you to the right trade. A plumber who tries to do everything is a plumber to question.

What Pennsauken homes mean for your plumbing

The median home in the township was built in 1964, according to American Community Survey data, and the oldest housing is in the south. Homes from that era often have cast iron drain lines, galvanized supply pipe in some cases, or copper added during later remodels. That mix is why a plumber should look at your actual pipes rather than guess from the address.

Most addresses get water from the Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission, and a few may be on New Jersey American Water, so check your bill.

Sewage is collected by the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority and treated by the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority. Most homes here are on public sewer, so a backup usually starts on your side of the connection but can involve the street main, and the plumber will tell you which.

Sources: American Community Survey housing data, Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission and Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority public pages

How to verify any New Jersey plumber

New Jersey licenses plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the Division of Consumer Affairs. State rules say plumbing may only be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor, or W-2 employees of one.

Advertising, invoices, and work vehicles are required to show the master plumber's name and license number. Ask for that number before work begins, then confirm it with the Board. This applies to us and to every other company you call.

Permits are a second check. Permits for plumbing work in Pennsauken are handled by the township Construction Code Department at 2400 Merchantville Ave, and the permit is signed by the licensed master plumber responsible for the job.

Before you let a plumber start

Run through this list while the plumber is still at the door.

  • Ask for the license number and write it down.
  • Confirm the name on the invoice or vehicle matches the license holder.
  • Get the price and the scope of work stated before any tools come out.
  • Ask whether a township permit is needed and who will pull it.
  • Know where your main water shut-off is so you can use it yourself.
  • Take photos of the problem and the damage before work begins.
  • Keep the invoice, since it records what was done and when.
