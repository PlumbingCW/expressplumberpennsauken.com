How Long Does It Take to Install a Water Heater in Pennsauken, NJ?

How long it takes depends on the job type more than the brand. A like-for-like tank swap is the shortest, because the gas line, vent or circuit and water connections already exist. A fuel change or a switch to tankless adds new piping, venting or wiring, and that work takes longer. We do not promise a number of hours, because access, the condition of old fittings and inspection timing all vary.

What we can do is describe the phases so you know what to expect in your Pennsauken, NJ home. Our water heater installation crew sorts the job type on the phone, then tells you how the day is likely to run before anyone arrives.

The phases of a water heater job

Think of the work in stages rather than hours. Each stage can go smoothly or can uncover something that needs attention.

First comes shutdown and draining, then removal of the old unit, then setting and connecting the new one, then filling, venting, testing and a check of the safety valve. A permit and an inspection sit around those steps when the work requires them.

Phone triage and job type

We ask about fuel, size, location and symptoms, then sort the job into like-for-like, conversion or tankless.

Shutoffs and draining

Power or gas is turned off, the cold supply is closed and the tank is emptied through a hose to a safe drain.

Removal and setting

The old unit comes out and the new one is set on a level base, with a drain pan where the space calls for it.

Connections and venting

Water lines, gas or electric, flue and relief valve piping are connected and checked.

Fill, test and inspect

The tank is filled, air is bled, controls are tested and the inspection is arranged if a permit applies.

Heat pump models are pickier about location. The Department of Energy lists a year-round temperature band and a minimum volume of surrounding air as requirements. A small or cold basement can turn a simple swap into a search for the right spot.

What can slow things down in older Pennsauken homes

The median home in Pennsauken was built around 1964, and the oldest housing stock sits toward the south of the township. Homes of that era often have older shutoff valves, galvanized fittings or flues that were sized for a previous heater.

When a valve will not close or a union breaks, the job adds a repair before the new unit goes in. That is not padding. It is the work that keeps the new heater from leaking on day one.

Permit and inspection timing in Pennsauken

Under the township's uniform construction code chapter, most construction needs a permit, with an exception for ordinary repairs. Plumbing permits come from the Construction Code Department, 2400 Merchantville Ave, phone 856-665-1000.

What this means for your schedule: if your job needs a permit and inspection, the work may be finished before the inspection happens. Ask the department how inspections are scheduled for your job so you know when to be home.

Source type: Township of Pennsauken municipal code, Chapter 133 (Uniform Construction Codes), and the Construction Code Department contact listing

What to prepare before the crew arrives

Prep list for install day

A clear path saves time in any job type.

  • Move stored items away from the heater and the path to it
  • Clear stairs and the route from the driveway to the basement
  • Know where your main water shutoff is
  • Keep pets in another room
  • Tell us if anyone in the house needs hot water for medical reasons
  • If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility or 911 first

Tankless installs and the gas line question

A tankless swap usually takes the longest of the common jobs, because venting and gas capacity are checked before anything is mounted. If the existing gas pipe is too small, it has to be upsized.
