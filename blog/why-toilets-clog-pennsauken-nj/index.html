Why Toilets Clog in Pennsauken, NJ: Causes and Fixes

Most toilet clogs come from what goes down the bowl: wipes, thick paper, hygiene products or a toy. Others come from the toilet itself, such as a low-flow bowl with weak flush power or a partly blocked trap. The rest come from outside the bathroom, in a vent that is blocked or a main line that is slowing everything down.

The fast test is simple. If only one toilet struggles and other drains run fine, the problem is local to that toilet. If tubs, sinks or floor drains gurgle or back up too, the blockage is in the line. For a stubborn bowl, our toilet repair team in Pennsauken, NJ can help by phone.

The usual suspects behind a clogged toilet

Flushing the wrong item is the leading cause, and the label on the package does not always help. Wipes marked flushable are a good example. The Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority says that even wipes labeled flushable do not break down, are a major cause of clogs, and that only toilet paper should be flushed.

The same CCMUA page lists paper towels, tissues, feminine hygiene products, cotton swabs, hair, kitty litter and grease among items that do not belong in drains or toilets.

Wipes and paper towels

They hold together in water and catch on the bend in the trap.

Too much paper at once

A heavy load can stall a bowl, especially a low-flow model.

Low-flow bowl wear

Mineral buildup in the rim holes weakens the flush.

Small objects

Toys, brush heads and cotton swabs lodge deep in the trap.

Blocked vent

A slow drain with gurgling may mean the vent stack is restricted.

Is it the toilet or the line?

Test the other fixtures before you start plunging. Run a bathroom sink and flush a second toilet if you have one. Watch the lowest drain in the house, often a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub.

If several fixtures react, the blockage is likely in the branch or the main line, and plunging the toilet will not fix it.

Plunger or auger: which tool for which clog

What you can try yourself

  • A flange plunger with a good seal on the drain
  • A toilet auger for a clog just past the bowl
  • Warm water and a little dish soap to loosen paper
  • Stopping the water supply if the bowl rises

When to call a plumber

  • More than one fixture is backing up
  • Sewage is coming up from a floor drain
  • The toilet clogs again within days
  • A toilet auger meets resistance or you suspect an object

Use a plunger gently at first, since an aggressive push can splash. An auger reaches past the bend, but it should be used with care to avoid scratching the porcelain. If a dropped object is lodged inside, forcing it can wedge it tighter.

Avoid chemical drain openers. Bowls and old pipes do not respond well to harsh products, and the chemicals are a hazard if the toilet has to be worked on afterward.

Older pipes and partial blockages

The median home in Pennsauken was built around 1964, and the oldest housing stock is in the south of the township. Homes of that era often have older drain lines that develop rough interiors or roots at joints, so a partial blockage keeps catching paper.

If a toilet clogs again and again even when you use little paper, the line may be the cause. Our drain cleaning team can run a camera and find the spot.

Pennsauken sewer system and what to flush

Most Pennsauken homes are on public sewer, collected by the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority and treated by the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority. CCMUA customer service can be reached at 856-541-3700 on weekdays, and its website lists guidance on what not to flush.

For you, the practical point is that a wipe that gets past your own trap can still snag in the public line downstream. If your own drain keeps clogging, call a plumber before assuming it is the street.

What to do while you wait

These steps protect your floor and keep the problem from growing.

  • Stop flushing and stop running water in the house
  • Lift the tank lid and set the float so the tank does not refill
  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet if the bowl is rising
  • Lay towels around the base and keep children away
  • Do not touch standing sewage with bare hands
  • Turn off power only if the area around outlets is dry and safe
