New Construction Basement Waterproofing in Pennsauken, NJ: The Plumbing Side

In a new basement, waterproofing is partly a plumbing job. The parts that must go in before the concrete is poured are the under-slab drain lines, the sump pit and its discharge, and any backwater valve on the sewer connection. Once the slab is poured, adding them means cutting concrete, so the plan has to be settled during rough-in.

This guide for Pennsauken, NJ builders and homeowners explains the plumbing side of a dry basement, the order of work, and where the township Construction Code Department fits in. Ask the department which inspections apply to your project, since requirements depend on the work.

What waterproofing means in a new basement

Waterproofing on a new foundation is a set of layers. The foundation wall gets its coating or membrane, and drainage collects water around the footing and under the slab. The plumbing side moves that water out through a sump pit and a discharge line, and keeps sewage from coming back in.

These layers work together. A perfect wall coating still fails if groundwater has nowhere to go, and a good sump pump still struggles if the drain system under the slab was left out. Plan the water path as a whole before the pour.

The plumbing pieces, in the order they go in

Under-slab drain lines

Before the slab goes down, drain lines are set in the gravel base. They carry the building's waste lines and, where planned, a perimeter drain that leads to the sump pit.

Sump pit rough-in

The pit is set in the gravel, level and at the right depth, with a sealed lid in mind. Water-collecting pipes tie into it before the concrete covers them.

Discharge line routing

The line that carries pumped water out must be planned to exit away from the foundation and the neighbor. It needs a check valve and a route that will not freeze shut.

Backwater valve on the sewer line

A backwater valve is installed on the building drain to stop sewage from flowing back from the street. It must be accessible for cleaning after the floor is finished.

Pump, power, and alarm

The pump needs a dedicated protected outlet. A battery backup and a high-water alarm are easy to plan now and awkward later.

Inspection timing and the Construction Code Department

In Pennsauken, permits for plumbing work are handled by the township Construction Code Department. The township code requires a permit for construction except ordinary repairs, and lists plumbing permits and fees. Ask the department which inspections apply to your project and in what order.

The practical rule is to get the answer before the pour. Underground work is hidden once concrete covers it, so inspection timing has to be known in advance. Your plumber or builder should schedule these steps and confirm with the department.

Questions to settle before the slab is poured

Bring this list to the plumber and to the township office.

  • Which inspections apply to the underground plumbing and in what order
  • Who pulls the permit and who is on site for the inspection
  • Where the sump pit, discharge line, and backwater valve will go
  • Whether the building drain connects to public sewer through the Sewerage Authority
  • Where the cleanouts will be after the floor is finished
  • Where the outlet and space for a backup pump will be

Sump, backwater valve, or both?

A sump pit and pump handle

  • Groundwater collected from the perimeter drain
  • Water rising under the slab
  • Water from storms that soaks into the soil

A backwater valve handles

  • Sewage flow coming back from the street main
  • Backups when the public sewer is overloaded
  • It does not remove groundwater

They solve different problems, which is why a new basement often gets both. Ready.gov advises installing check valves, a related device, as part of flood readiness. Your plumber can explain how a valve on the building drain would work with your fixtures.

A plumber doing plumbing installation can plan the rough-in for a new basement, and basement waterproofing advice covers how the drain system fits with the wall and floor.

What Pennsauken builders and owners should know about permits and sewers

The Construction Code Department is at 2400 Merchantville Ave in Pennsauken, and the plumbing subcode office is reachable at 856-665-1000, extension 1406. The township code ties some approvals to the Camden County Department of Health, the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority, and the water provider. Ask which of these your project needs.

Most Pennsauken homes are on public sewer, with local collection by the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority and treatment by CCMUA. That makes a backwater valve a conversation to have with the department and your plumber, not an assumption.

Public pages from CCMUA also describe sewer separation work in the township. That is a utility project with no published effect on individual lots, so ask the Sewerage Authority what, if anything, it means for your street before you design the drain connection.

Standards and rules behind this advice

The New Jersey Uniform Construction Code governs permits and inspections for new plumbing, and Ready.gov flood guidelines recommend check valves and a backup pump.
