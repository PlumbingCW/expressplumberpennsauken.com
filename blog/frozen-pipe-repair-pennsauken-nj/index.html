Frozen Pipe Repair in Pennsauken, NJ: Thaw Safely, Know When It Split

If a faucet gives only a trickle in freezing weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Open that faucet, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or heating pad, and never use a torch or any open flame. If the pipe is already cracked or you see water spraying, close the main water valve first and call the 24/7 line.

A frozen pipe is not yet a burst pipe, and that gap is your chance to act. Ice blocks flow and builds pressure, and the split often happens as the ice melts and water moves again. Below: where pipes freeze in Pennsauken, NJ, how to thaw them, and the point where you should stop and call.

Where pipes freeze in a Pennsauken house

The National Weather Service defines a freeze as air temperature below 32 degrees Fahrenheit. Pipes do not freeze the moment the air hits that mark, but long cold spells and wind get them there. The risky spots are the ones the heat does not reach.

Census survey data puts the median year built for Pennsauken homes at 1964. Homes of that era often have supply lines in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and unfinished basements, which are the usual trouble spots.

Exterior walls

A line run against an outside wall sits next to the cold. Kitchen and bath sinks on exterior walls are the classic example.

Crawlspaces and basement corners

Where the water service enters through the foundation, the pipe is exposed to cold air leaking in. A gap in the rim joist can be enough.

Garages and attached sheds

Water lines that cross an unheated garage are exposed when the door stays open or the space is not insulated.

Hose bibs

A hose left connected traps water in the spigot and the pipe behind it. The freeze often splits the pipe inside the wall, where you cannot see it.

Signs a pipe has frozen

  • Only a trickle comes out of a faucet, or nothing at all.
  • Frost is visible on a pipe in a crawlspace or basement.
  • One fixture is dead while others work, which points to a local freeze.
  • A pipe feels extremely cold, or bulges slightly.
  • You hear water hissing or dripping near a frozen section after it starts to thaw.

How to thaw a frozen pipe step by step

Open the faucet

Leave the tap open so water can flow as the ice melts. Moving water also helps clear the blockage.

Apply gentle heat

The American Red Cross lists an electric hair dryer, a heating pad, a portable space heater kept away from anything flammable, or towels soaked in hot water. Keep heat on until full pressure returns.

Never use open flame

Do not use a blowtorch, propane or kerosene heater, or any open flame device. Fire risk, scorched framing and pipe damage are all real.

Check the other taps

One frozen pipe often means others are frozen too. Test every faucet before you assume the problem is solved.

Stop and call

If you cannot find the frozen section, cannot reach it or cannot thaw it, call a licensed plumber rather than escalating the heat.

When a frozen pipe has already split

A split pipe can sit quiet until the ice melts, then leak heavily. If you see water on a ceiling, a wet wall or a puddle near a once-frozen pipe, treat it as a burst. Close the main shutoff, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, and keep clear of wet outlets.

A fresh split is the work of burst pipe repair, which usually means cutting out the damaged section and replacing it. Do not wait for the leak to slow down, because lines under pressure rarely stop on their own.

Preventing the next freeze

Before the next cold snap

These steps come from Red Cross winter guidance and basic plumbing practice.

  • Close the inside valve to each outdoor hose bib, then open the outside spigot so the line drains.
  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawlspaces and under cabinets. Pipe sleeves or UL listed heat cable can help.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • In very cold weather, let cold water drip from faucets fed by exposed pipes.
  • Keep the thermostat steady day and night, and if you travel, keep the heat on at no lower than 55 degrees Fahrenheit.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and that every adult in the house can find it.

After a freeze, a slow leak may follow. If your meter keeps moving once everything is off, leak detection can trace the loss before it damages drywall.

The commission lists a 24-hour operator for water emergencies at (856) 663-0044 and an office line at (856) 663-0043 on weekdays. If water is coming up in the street or the whole block is dry, that is a utility problem and not a pipe in your house.

The commission's website does not give shutoff valve instructions, so find your own main before winter. Look where the service line enters the house, often near the foundation, which is also a common freeze spot. Knowing its location turns a flood into a short mess.
