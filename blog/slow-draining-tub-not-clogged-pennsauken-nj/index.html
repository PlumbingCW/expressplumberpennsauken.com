Slow-Draining Tub That Is Not Clogged: What to Check in Pennsauken, NJ

When a tub drains slowly and there is no hair in sight, the cause is usually one of four things. It may be a stopper that does not fully lift, buildup inside the trap, a venting problem, or a partial blockage further down the line. The quickest way to tell them apart is to run other fixtures and see whether they are slow too.

If only the tub is slow, the problem is local to the tub drain. If the sink, shower or toilet also lag or gurgle, the blockage is shared and sits in a branch or the main line. This guide covers how to test, what to try, and when a Pennsauken, NJ crew in Camden County should take over.

First, test whether it is only the tub

Before touching the tub, run the other fixtures. Fill the bathroom sink and let it go, flush the toilet and watch the bowl, and check any floor drain or laundry standpipe in the house. Notice any gurgle as one fixture drains.

One slow tub with every other fixture normal points to the tub itself. Two or more slow fixtures point to a shared line. Water backing up in a basement floor drain while the tub runs is a stronger sign still.

The stopper and linkage: the hidden culprit

Many tubs use a pop-up stopper or a trip lever inside the overflow plate. When the linkage drifts out of adjustment, the plug rises only partway. Water squeezes past a half-open plug and the tub drains as if partly clogged.

A trip-lever tub has a rod and plunger behind the overflow plate. Pull the plate and the assembly out, wipe off the soap film and check that the plunger moves freely. A stiff or kinked linkage is a repair, and a bent rod is cheap to replace.

Unscrew and lift the stopper

Remove it, clean the stem and look for a build-up of soap scum or hair wrapped around the post.

Check the overflow plate

Take off the plate and withdraw the linkage. Clean it, then reinsert and test whether the plug opens its full range.

Flush with hot water

Run hot tap water for a few minutes. Avoid caustic drain openers, which can damage older pipes and splash on whoever works on the line next.

Retest the drain speed

Fill the tub a few inches and time how it empties compared with a few days earlier. A big change means the stopper was the cause.

Tub still slow after the stopper check?

If the linkage is clean and the tub still lags, the trap or branch line is next. Call the 24/7 line if water is rising anywhere.

Venting and trap problems

Drain pipes need air behind the water to flow freely. A plumbing vent that is blocked by a nest or debris on the roof, or a pipe that has been altered, can slow drainage and cause gurgling. The tub drains in a slow, bubbling rhythm and the toilet may burble as well.

The trap under the tub is another suspect. It catches hair and soap, and a lining of buildup narrows the passage even when nothing looks plugged. This is where a snake or a drain machine reaches farther than a hand tool. Our drain cleaning work covers branch lines from the tub trap outward.

A partial main line blockage

A main line that is partly blocked lets water through slowly. Fixtures far from the main, or on the lowest floor, show it first. Grease, roots, a collapsed section or a cracked pipe can all be the cause.

Signs to watch are several slow drains at once, a toilet bubbling when the tub runs and sewage odor near a basement floor drain. Stop running water in the house when you see these, because the next load can push waste back out of the lowest opening.

Pennsauken sewer: who handles which part

Pennsauken Township collects sewage through the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority, and the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority treats it. Most Pennsauken homes are on public sewer rather than septic, so a main line problem usually involves the pipe from your house to the street connection. The CCMUA customer service line is 856-541-3700, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Ask the Sewerage Authority which section of the pipe belongs to you before paying for a repair near the street.

The street main is a public matter, while the pipe on your property is usually the owner's, so confirm the dividing point. A camera inspection can show which side the blockage is on. Census survey data puts the median year built in the township at 1964, and homes of that era often have older drain materials that are worth a camera check.

What you can try yourself

  • Test other fixtures for slowness and gurgling.
  • Clean the stopper, overflow plate and linkage.
  • Flush with hot water and use a plastic hair tool.
  • Check for a vent cap that is blocked, only if you can do so safely from the ground.

When to call a plumber

  • More than one fixture is slow, or a floor drain backs up.
  • The tub gurgles or the toilet bubbles when water runs.
  • The slow drain returns within days of cleaning. A cleaning visit should include a look at the line.
  • A drip at the spout or diverter, which is a separate fix handled by faucet repair.

Notes that help the plumber

Details speed up the diagnosis.

  • Which fixtures are slow, and since when.
  • Any gurgling, bubbling or odor and where it is loudest.
  • Whether the problem is worse after laundry or a long shower.
  • What you already tried, including any chemical cleaners.
  • Which floor the tub is on and whether a basement floor drain exists.
