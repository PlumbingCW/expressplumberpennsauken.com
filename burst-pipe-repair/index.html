Burst pipe emergency: stop the water in this order

The goal in the first few minutes is simple. Stop the flow, protect the electrical system, and keep the damage from spreading. Everything else can wait until the water is off.

Find and close the main valve

Most homes have it where the line enters the basement or utility room, often near the meter. Turn a round handle clockwise, or turn a lever a quarter turn until it sits across the pipe.

Drain the lines

Open the lowest faucet in the house, usually in the basement or on the first floor, and let the water run out. Flush a toilet once to empty the tank.

Cut power to a wet area only if it is safe

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach the panel on dry floor, and call the 24/7 line if you cannot.

Turn off the water heater

Switch off an electric heater at its breaker, or set a gas unit to pilot or off. A heater that loses its supply should not keep heating an empty or half-empty tank.

Call and describe what you see

Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from, whether the main valve closed fully, and if any ceiling is sagging.

If the problem is on the street side

The street valve (curb stop) and the meter belong to the utility, not to you. If the leak is between the street and the meter, or the main valve in your house will not stop the flow, call Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission's water emergencies line at (856) 663-0044. Customers on New Jersey American Water should call that utility instead, so check your bill.

What you can do safely

  • Shut the main valve and drain the lines.
  • Move belongings, rugs and electronics out of the water's path.
  • Photograph the pipe and the damage before cleanup.
  • Soak up standing water with towels, a mop or a wet vacuum on a dry outlet.

When to call a plumber right away

  • The main valve will not turn or does not stop the flow.
  • Water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel.
  • A ceiling bulges or drips from a hidden source.
  • You smell gas or see signs of sewage mixed in.
  • The pipe is cracked in a spot you cannot reach.

Cracked or broken pipes: what failed and how it is fixed

A pipe rarely fails for one reason alone. Pressure, corrosion, a hard knock, vibration, or a freeze each leave a different mark, and the right repair depends on which one happened. A plumber looks at the break first, then at the length of pipe around it.

A single clean split in good pipe may need only a short section replaced. If the nearby pipe is thin, pitted or flaking, the better fix is a longer run, because patching one weak spot often just moves the next failure down the line.

Copper pipe repair

Copper is common in homes of that era, and it holds up well when conditions are right. Pinholes tend to appear where water chemistry, flow speed or a poor joint wears at one spot. A green or white crust on the outside is an early warning worth showing us before it becomes a spray.

Pipe leak repair and drain pipe repair

A dripping supply joint is a different job from a cracked drain. Supply lines are under constant pressure, so even small drips rise to an emergency if they sit above finished ceilings. Drain lines carry waste, so a crack there means water that is not clean, and we ask you to avoid contact.

When the broken pipe is a drain, the repair is cut-and-couple or a replaced section, plus a check that the slope still carries water away. If a drain breaks under a slab or in the yard, see our sewer line repair page for what that involves.

Frozen pipes: thaw them safely

A frozen pipe may not have burst yet, but it can when it thaws. If a faucet gives you a trickle or nothing in cold weather, assume a freeze. Shut the main water valve, open the affected faucet, and warm the area gently.

Use a hair dryer on low or warm towels, moving from the faucet toward the frozen section. Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame. Metal conducts heat quickly, and flame has started fires inside walls. If you cannot find the frozen spot or the pipe is already split, call the 24/7 line.

Pipes most at risk sit along exterior walls, in unheated crawlspaces and basements, and near rim joists. Keeping cabinet doors open on cold nights and sealing air gaps helps. We do not quote how deep the ground freezes here, since it varies by season, so ask the township or the utility for local guidance if you are planning an outdoor line.

Water damage first steps after the pipe is stopped

Do this once the water is off

Speed matters, because wet drywall and insulation can grow mold if left damp.

  • Photograph every wet surface and the broken pipe before moving anything.
  • Remove standing water and open windows to move air if the weather allows.
  • Lift wet rugs and move furniture to dry ground.
  • Do not turn the main back on until the pipe has been repaired and tested.
  • Call your insurer, and ask about dry-out help if drywall or floors are soaked.
  • Keep the broken pipe section if possible, since it shows the cause.
