A historic neighborhood on the Merchantville border

A homes.com neighborhood guide describes Collins Tract as a historic neighborhood of Pennsauken established in 1886, with a median year built of 1928. It shares the Centre Street area and some amenities with Merchantville. The guide mentions Collins Tract Park and describes quiet, tree-lined streets with oak and maple trees.

Streets that appear in Collins Tract property listings include Collins Avenue, Rogers Avenue, Githens Avenue, Harvey Avenue, Walnut Avenue and North Centre Street. A local school history also names Collins Tract: one of the two schools opened in 1927 served Delaware Gardens, Hillcrest and the west part of Collins Tract.

The boundary with Merchantville is not marked on most maps. Merchantville is a separate borough, so a Pennsauken Township address uses Pennsauken's permit office, and a Merchantville borough address uses the borough's. Confirm which one is on your mail before you ask about a permit.

Older homes: look first, assume nothing

Property guides describe Victorian-style houses, bungalows of roughly 1,300 to 1,500 square feet and larger colonials of 1,500 to 3,000 square feet. A house built in the 1920s may have had its plumbing changed several times since. Age is a reason to inspect, not a diagnosis.

Mature trees are part of the appeal of these streets. As a general point, tree roots can enter joints in any older sewer lateral, wherever it is. A camera run is the way to know, and sewer line repair options range from a spot repair to relining depending on what the camera shows.

Utilities, permits and renovation

Many Collins Tract houses are being updated, and plumbing work during a remodel usually needs a permit. Pennsauken's township code requires a permit for construction work other than ordinary repairs, and the plumbing subcode official reviews plumbing work. Our plumbing installation page explains how the township process works, and a new tank or tankless unit is covered under water heater installation.
