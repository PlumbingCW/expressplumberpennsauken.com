Service line leak emergency: signs and first moves

Your service line is the underground pipe between the utility's connection near the street and your meter or foundation. When it leaks, the water often goes down instead of up, so the evidence is indirect. A leak detection visit can confirm it before any digging.

Wet or sunken ground

A patch of lawn that stays soft, a sinking driveway edge or a bubbling sidewalk joint can mark the spot.

A meter that keeps turning

If the dial moves with every fixture off and the leak stops when the indoor main closes, the loss sits between the meter and the house.

Pressure that fades

Showers that weaken and faucets that sputter, with no clog, can mean water is leaving the line before it reaches you.

Muddy or rust-tinted water

Sediment pulled into a broken line, or disturbed rust in an old steel pipe, can tint the tap.

If water is flooding a basement or pooling at the foundation, shut the main valve and call right away. After-hours calls get the same triage as daytime ones, and we schedule a same-day visit when availability allows. If it looks like a break at the street valve or on the utility's side of the meter, call MPWC water emergencies at (856) 663-0044.

What your service line is made of, and why age matters

Service lines depend on age. A line installed decades ago is likely a different material from one put in during a recent upgrade, and the material shapes both the failure and the repair. You cannot tell by looking at the yard, but the pipe where it enters your basement often reveals it.

Service line materials and what to expect from each
MaterialWhere it shows upWhat to know
CopperCommon in homes updated or built in the copper eraDurable, but can pit or split, and joints may fail over time
Galvanized steelOlder homes of the mid-century eraRusts from the inside, narrows the bore and weakens the walls
LeadPossible in the oldest service linesNeeds special attention, covered in the next section
PEX or HDPENewer or replaced linesFlexible, resists corrosion, requires correct fittings and bedding

Lead service line replacement

Some older service lines contain lead, and the water utility is the best source on what is known about yours. MPWC publishes a Lead Service Line inventory and FAQ resources at mpwc.com. Ask them how it applies to your address, and whether the line on their side and the line on your side have been identified.

If your line is lead or suspected lead, a plumber can replace the homeowner side with a modern material. Coordinating with the utility matters, because a partial swap should be planned with them first. We explain the plan and the permit steps before we begin.

Low water pressure: how to tell if the line is the cause

Not every pressure problem is a leak or a bad service line. A short checklist rules out the simple causes before anyone digs.

  • Check whether low pressure hits every fixture or only one. A single weak tap is usually an aerator or cartridge, not the main.
  • Look at the main valve and any meter-side valve. A gate valve that is only partly open throttles the whole house.
  • Find the pressure regulator, if there is one, a bell-shaped brass fitting near the main. A tired regulator can cut flow or let pressure spike.
  • Think about galvanized plumbing. Rust buildup narrows older steel pipe, so flow drops gradually at the farthest fixtures.
  • Ask a neighbor, or the utility, whether pressure is low on your street. That points away from your own line.

If those checks come up clean, a pressure gauge on a hose bib shows what the house is getting, and a leak test finds out whether the service line is losing water. Replacing a failing line or fixing a regulator often restores the flow in one visit.

Repair or replace the line?

A spot repair fits when

  • One clear leak sits in an otherwise sound pipe.
  • The pipe material is in good condition at the cut.
  • The leak is in a place that can be reached with a small excavation.
  • There is no history of earlier leaks along the same run.

Replacement is the better call when

  • The line is old galvanized steel or suspected lead.
  • Repairs have already been made once or twice.
  • The pipe shows pitting, flaking or heavy buildup inside.
  • Pressure has been poor for a long time and a repair would not fix it.

Trench or trenchless: how the work gets done

A trench is the standard method. We dig down, expose the pipe, replace or repair it, and backfill. It gives full access and lets us inspect the bedding, but it disturbs the lawn, walkway or pavement above.

Trenchless methods, like pulling or bursting a new pipe through the old path, can reduce surface damage. They are not suitable for every site, since depth, soil and the path of the old line all matter. We tell you which one fits your property before we price it.

Where the utility ends and your pipe begins

The curb stop at the street and the water meter belong to the utility. In Pennsauken that is usually the Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission at 6751 Westfield Ave, though a few addresses are on New Jersey American Water, so check your bill. What affects the price of water line work

Service line jobs vary more than almost any plumbing task, because most of the cost is hidden underground.

Length and depth of the lineA longer run or a deeper pipe means more digging or a longer pull.
MaterialCopper, PEX and HDPE are priced differently, as are the fittings that go with them.
Surface to restoreLawn is simpler to put back than concrete, pavers or asphalt.
MethodOpen trench and trenchless work use different equipment and labor.
Utility coordination and permitsSome jobs require inspections or tie-ins handled by the utility or the township.

Before the crew arrives

A little preparation keeps the visit short and the digging in the right place.

  • Clear the path between the street and the house, including cars, pavers and planters.
  • Know where the main shut-off valve is inside the house.
  • Locate any irrigation heads, invisible fence wires or buried tanks.
  • Have a recent water bill handy, so we can see which utility serves you.
  • Plan for a short water shutoff, and fill a few containers if you need them.

If you are unsure whether the leak is in the service line at all, our leak detection work comes first. For breaks inside the house, see burst pipe repair.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Components that touch drinking water are certified to NSF/ANSI 61, and the federal Lead and Copper Rule governs how water systems deal with lead service lines. The Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission publishes a lead service line inventory and FAQ, so ask it how the rules apply to your address.
