How a plumbing installation job usually runs

Walk the plan

We start with what you want: new fixtures, a moved sink, a new bath or a line to an appliance. Photos and a sketch help on the phone.

Check what exists

Pipe material, shutoffs, drain slopes and vent paths decide what the new work can tie into.

Permit and schedule

The permit is filed with the township, and rough-in is planned before walls close.

Rough-in and inspection

Pipes are run, tested and inspected before drywall or flooring covers them.

Set fixtures and final

Fixtures go on, the system is checked, and the final inspection closes the permit.

Remodel rough-in and relocating fixtures

Rough-in is the pipe work that happens inside open walls and floors. It is the best time to move a sink, add a shower or upgrade a stack, since the cost of changing it later is far higher.

Moving a fixture is mostly a question of drains. Water lines are flexible, but drains need slope and a vent, so a long move can mean opening a floor or ceiling.

New construction plumbing

On a new build or addition, plumbing is planned before the slab or framing. We coordinate the layout of drains under the floor, stacks through the walls and supply runs to each fixture. Permits and inspections in Pennsauken

Permits for plumbing work are handled by the township Construction Code Department at 2400 Merchantville Ave. The plumbing permit fee depends on the type of work, so see the township fee schedule for current amounts.

Approvals can involve the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority and the water provider, depending on the job. Ask the department what applies to your project before work begins. An inspection is scheduled after rough-in, and you will want pipes visible for it.

Check the license

Under New Jersey rules, plumbing is performed by a master plumber, an authorized contractor, or employees of one. Ask any plumber for the license number and verify it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers.

Gas line installation: safety comes first

If you smell gas

Leave the building at once and do not touch switches or flames. From outside, call the gas utility or 911. Plumbers do not replace that call, and we make no promise about a gas response time.

New gas piping for a range, dryer, grill or water heater needs a permit, pressure testing and inspection. Never connect gas appliances yourself, and never skip the test.

Before adding a gas appliance, we check the capacity of the existing line. Fuel changes are covered under water heater installation.

Water softener and filtration: what to know first

Softeners, filters and conditioners do different jobs. A softener removes hardness minerals with salt, a filter removes particles, taste or chlorine, and a conditioner changes how scale forms without removing it.

Start with your water. Ask Merchantville Pennsauken Water Commission, or NJ American Water if that is your provider, for the annual water quality report, and test your tap. Check your bill to see which utility serves you. Then choose equipment for the problem you actually have.

Standards and rules behind this advice

Plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the Pennsauken Construction Code Department enforces locally. Gas piping is covered by NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and electrical work follows the National Electrical Code (NEC). A permit and inspection are the normal route for new installation.
