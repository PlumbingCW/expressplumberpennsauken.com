A borough built in layers: what each era hides

Collingswood was incorporated in 1888 and grew fastest between 1900 and 1920. By 1930 the population stood near 12,700, up from a few hundred in 1890. A large share of the housing therefore dates from the early twentieth century, and plumbing from that period was made of different materials than a house built last decade.

Emergency plumbing in Collingswood: sewer, steel pipe and tank failure

Sewer backing up from the lowest drain

A basement floor drain or first-floor tub that backs up usually means the lateral or the street line is blocked. Stop flushing, avoid contact with the water, and call. A recorded sewer camera inspection shows whether roots, a crack or a belly in the line is behind it.

A galvanized line that has started to leak

Rust-thinned steel pipe can weep, then let go at a threaded joint. Shut the main and open a low faucet to relieve the line. We repair the section, and a plumber can tell you whether the rest of the run deserves replacement.

A basement water heater failing

Cold showers, rumbling or water at the base of the tank all mean the unit is near the end. Turn off its fuel or power and close the cold supply. Same-day water heater replacement is possible when parts and a plumber are available.

Sewage or steel pipe giving way?

The 24/7 line is answered at night and on weekends. Name the drain that is backing up or the spot where water is coming through.

Collingswood water and sewer run through the borough

The Borough of Collingswood Water/Sewer Department supplies the water, drawn from seven wells in the Raritan aquifer, and maintains the sanitary and storm sewer lines under the streets. The Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority treats the sewage, so a homeowner may receive two sewer bills. The borough also flushes hydrants from time to time, which can discolor water briefly.

Cloudy water that clears after a minute at the tap on a flushing day comes from the main. If it keeps returning, or only the hot side is affected, the cause is in the house.

Have this ready when the plumber is on the way

A few minutes of preparation saves time at the door.

  • Find and mark the master shutoff, usually where the line enters the house near the meter.
  • Turn off a failing water heater's power or gas control, and leave it off.
  • Clear the path to the basement, cleanout and heater.
  • Note which drains are slow and which ones gurgle, since it points to where the blockage sits.
  • Photograph any water damage.

Rules and licensing in this town

Plumbing permits and inspections in Collingswood go through the local construction code office under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code, and New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or that contractor's employees.
