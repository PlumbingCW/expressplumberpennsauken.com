When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow bathroom sink is an annoyance. Water coming back up a tub while the washer runs, or a basement floor drain that bubbles, means the blockage sits in a shared line. The next load of water has nowhere to go.

Treat these as urgent: sewage smell with standing water, several fixtures backing up, a toilet that rises when you drain the sink, or an overflow heading toward finished floors or electrical gear. Nights and weekends get the same attention, with same-day appointments when the schedule allows.

  • Stop using every sink, shower and washer until the line is open.
  • If the water is dirty, keep children and pets out of it and wear gloves for cleanup.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a standing backup. They can splash back and they make the later work riskier.
  • If wet floors are near a panel or outlets, stay clear and shut the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Snake, cable or jet: which tool clears your drain

The words get mixed up, so here is the plain version. A hand snake is a coiled spring for a single fixture trap. A power cable is a longer steel line with a cutting head that spins through a branch or main. Jetting pushes high-pressure water through the pipe and washes the walls.

The honest answer depends on what is in the pipe. A hair clog right under a tub drain does not need a jetter, and a wall of old grease will beat a small cable every time.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food scraps, the disposal and the dishwasher line

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom ones. Cooking fat runs through hot, then cools into a paste that narrows the pipe a little more with every dish load. Rice, pasta and coffee grounds swell and pack into that paste, and soon the sink drains in slow motion.

A garbage disposal adds its own twist. Ground waste is finer, but it settles in the trap and the first horizontal run. If the second basin fills when the first one drains, the blockage is usually past the disposal outlet rather than inside it.

The dishwasher drain hose often joins the disposal or the sink tailpiece, so a clog there shows up as dirty water sitting in the dishwasher bottom. We check that connection before cabling, because it changes where the machine goes in. For a stuck or leaking disposal itself, see our garbage disposal service.

Shower and tub drains that will not keep up

Shower and tub clogs are mostly hair bound with soap film, sitting just below the strainer or in the trap. Many are cleared from the overflow plate or the drain opening with a small auger. If it returns within weeks, the real clog is deeper, and a camera tells us whether the branch itself is narrowed.

Slow drain or a leak in the drain line?

Not every slow drain is a clog. A cracked or loose drain pipe can leak into a wall or crawl space, leaving a damp stain, a musty smell, or a floor that stays wet. Homes in Pennsauken, NJ have a median build year near 1964, and houses of that era often have cast iron or galvanized drain sections that rust at the joints.

If a stain stays after the drain is cleared, ask for a camera look. Our leak detection page covers the hidden-water side.

Emergency checklist before the plumber arrives

A few quick steps make the first visit shorter.

  • Note which fixtures are slow or backed up and which still drain.
  • Find the main cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement or a yard riser.
  • Clear the area around the sink or floor drain so a machine can be set up.
  • Remove stored boxes from the basement drain zone.
  • Write down what went down the drain recently, including any chemicals you tried.

If the clog is in the main in Pennsauken, NJ

If a branch is cleared and trouble keeps returning, ask who owns the pipe where the water is stalling. Pennsauken sewage goes first to the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority system. When several neighbors are slow at once, a short call to the Authority can save you from paying to dig your own line for a main problem.

Storm drains, gutters and yard drains

Rain gutters, downspout leaders, yard drains and basement catch basins carry stormwater, not household waste, and they clog with leaves, shingle grit and silt. We clear private yard and catch basin lines on request. A public storm inlet in the street is a different matter, so contact the township if one near your home is blocked or flooding.
